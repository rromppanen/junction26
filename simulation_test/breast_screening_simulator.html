What would closing each county's mammography attendance gap be worth? Screening helps only by moving diagnoses to earlier stages, so raising attendance shifts each county's stage mix from the non-attender mix towards the attender mix. Adapted from Shafik et al. (2022). Results are per year at steady state, for one year of diagnoses, undiscounted.

stage mixcounty = attendance × mixattenders + (1 − attendance) × mixnon-attenders  ·  ICER = Δcost ÷ Δlife-years

Illustrative inputs. Attendance and population are observed Sotkanet data (2019–2024). Every assumption marked placeholder is illustrative and must be replaced with sourced values before any result is quoted.

Value of closing each county's gap

Net cost = extra screens + outreach + change in treatment costs (negative means savings from fewer late-stage cases). Life-years and deaths refer to one year of diagnoses; the gains are realised over the following years. Cost per life-year is the same in every county because all counties share one set of assumptions and the model is linear. Counties differ in how much there is to gain.

How uncertain is it?

Limitations to carry into the pitch

  • Placeholders. Every stage-mix, survival, cost and incidence number is illustrative until replaced with Finnish Cancer Registry, NORDCAN, Statistics Finland or Lehtinen et al. values.
  • Ecological data. These are county averages. Low attendance correlates with a larger foreign-background population across counties (Spearman −0.79), but that is a hypothesis about who doesn't attend, not proof.
  • Self-selection. Women who skip screening may differ in other ways too, so the true effect of making them attend is probably smaller than the raw stage difference. As in Shafik et al., there is no natural-history model.
  • Same simplifications as the paper. Survival and unit costs are fixed per stage. No QALYs, no discounting and no false-positive harms; overdiagnosis is only a crude extra-cases term.
  • Scope. Steady state, ages 50–69 only, one year of diagnoses, biennial screening.
